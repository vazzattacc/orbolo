The Orbolo Ecosystem Protocol

Our compass for conducting business that serves life in all its forms.

Introduction

Orbolo restores ecosystems, uplifts communities, and creates a world where people
and planet thrive together. We operate through regenerative agriculture, ranching, and
nature-based solutions that generate lasting value while building climate-resilient
landscapes. This protocol deﬁnes how we work across our governance, labor
standards, ecosystem management practices, ﬁnancial commitments, partnerships,
compliance measures, and impact metrics. It guides our operations across all
geographies and ensures alignment with global standards while respecting local
contexts.

Disclaimer

This is a living document that evolves as we learn and grow. Our core philosophy—
restoring ecosystems and uplifting communities—remains unwavering.

I. FOUNDATIONAL PRINCIPLES: THE L.I.F.E. PHILOSOPHY

Orbolo operates on four interconnected principles that guide every decision and action:

Leverage: We harness the power of nature, innovation, partnerships, and local
networks to unlock meaningful, scalable solutions.

Invest: We channel investment into people, ecosystems, and sustainable solutions—
because long-term impact must also be ﬁnancially viable.

Fortify: We strengthen biodiversity, soil health, and climate resilience, laying the
foundation for regenerative land use and thriving landscapes.

Educate: We educate to empower a new generation of change-makers and land
stewards who drive and sustain transformation from the ground up.

These principles uphold human rights and dignity through ILO core conventions,
practice environmental stewardship by respecting planetary boundaries, maintain
transparency and accountability by measuring and reporting our actions, and create
intergenerational equity by generating value for current and future generations.

III. LABOR RELATIONS & HUMAN CAPITAL

We treat people with dignity and create pathways for growth. Our employment
standards provide fair wages, safe working conditions, and respect for labor rights. We
invest in people's development through training opportunities and career progression
pathways that enable advancement. We prioritize local employment and meaningful
community participation, ensuring our presence strengthens rather than disrupts local
economies. We build diverse teams and eliminate discrimination through active
commitment to gender equity and inclusion. We provide accessible grievance
mechanisms that allow anyone to raise and resolve concerns without fear of retaliation.

IV. COMMUNITY RELATIONS & INDIGENOUS PEOPLES

We recognize and respect the rights, knowledge, and cultural heritage of local and
indigenous communities. We commit to Free, Prior and Informed Consent (FPIC) before
any activities aGecting indigenous territories or traditional lands. We prioritize learning
from local and traditional ecological knowledge, integrating it with scientiﬁc
approaches. We ensure meaningful participation of local communities in decision-
making processes aGecting their lands and livelihoods. We respect territorial claims
and work transparently with all stakeholders to resolve any disputes through dialogue
and mediation. We build partnerships with local organizations to ensure culturally
appropriate engagement. We create economic opportunities that beneﬁt local
communities without displacing traditional practices or populations. We document and
protect traditional knowledge with full consent and beneﬁt-sharing agreements. Our
approach is guided by mutual respect, continuous learning, and the principle that
thriving ecosystems require thriving communities.

V. BIOSAFETY & GENETICALLY MODIFIED ORGANISMS

We prohibit artiﬁcial genetic modiﬁcation of all organisms. We use only non-GMO
seeds, native species, and naturally adapted varieties. We implement biosafety
measures aligned with the Cartagena Protocol on Biosafety and organic certiﬁcation
standards. Our approach prioritizes math and data-driven solutions over chemical
interventions, eliminating synthetic pesticides, herbicides, and fertilizers. We protect
biodiversity by using native and naturally adapted species that have evolved within their
ecosystems. We maintain seed sovereignty through preservation and propagation of
traditional and open-pollinated varieties. We establish buGer zones to prevent GMO
contamination from neighboring properties. We work with natural biological
processes—soil microbiomes, beneﬁcial insects, and plant symbiotic relationships—

rather than artiﬁcial genetic manipulation. Our biosafety protocols ensure all inputs,
seeds, and livestock breeds are veriﬁed non-GMO and support ecosystem health. This
commitment protects both environmental integrity and the health of communities
consuming our products.

VI. ECOSYSTEM MANAGEMENT CODE: NATURE & HUMAN PILARS

Pilar Nature

We manage land to regenerate ecosystems through integrated practices. We enhance
biodiversity, soil biology, structure, and carbon content to build living soil systems. We
restore hydrological cycles and improve water retention, recognizing water as the
foundation of life. We increase species diversity and ecosystem complexity,
understanding that biodiversity creates resilience. We capture and store atmospheric
carbon in soil and biomass, contributing to climate stability. We apply holistic
management principles based on methods such as Savory and Carvalho, using
regenerative grazing to heal landscapes. We integrate trees, crops, and livestock
through agroforestry and orcharding, creating resilient multi-layered systems. We track
ecosystem health using sensors, drones, and satellites, generating data that informs
decisions. We learn continuously and adjust practices based on results through
adaptive management cycles.

Animal Welfare Standards

We treat all animals as evolution intended. They live the best possible lives, closest to
what they evolved to do, in their natural social structures. This approach maximizes
their contribution to ecosystem health while honoring their intrinsic value. We uphold
the Five Freedoms: freedom from hunger and thirst, freedom from discomfort, freedom
from pain, injury, and disease, freedom from fear and distress, and freedom to express
normal behavior.

Pilar Humans

We build human capacity and economic opportunity through four channels. We share
knowledge and develop skills through education. We strengthen social bonds and local
leadership through community engagement. We develop and deploy regenerative
technologies through innovation. We create viable markets for regenerative products
through commercialization.

X. MONITORING, REPORTING & CONTINUOUS IMPROVEMENT

We redeﬁne KPIs to measure the change and the impact we want to implement, for
example:

Brain Power Per Hectare: Number of trained agroecological practitioners per hectare,
hours of skilled attention and decision-making applied annually, integration of human
intuition with technology systems, and local knowledge holders actively engaged in land
management.

Nutrient Density Over Weight: Nutrient content per unit of food produced, soil biology
indicators correlating with nutrient density, premium pricing achieved through quality
diGerentiation, and health outcomes in communities consuming our products.

Biomass Produced: Total organic matter produced per hectare annually, above and
below-ground biomass including root systems, carbon sequestration through biomass
accumulation, and biodiversity supported through habitat creation.

Margin Over Yield: Proﬁt margin per hectare after all costs, revenue per hectare from
diversiﬁed income streams, cost eGiciency through reduced external inputs, and
premium pricing through quality and sustainability certiﬁcation.

Agroecological Ecosystem Diversity: Human diversity (skills variety, age groups,
cultural backgrounds, knowledge systems), plant diversity (native species count, crop

varieties, functional plant groups), animal diversity (wild species abundance, livestock
breeds, beneﬁcial insects, soil organisms), crop diversity (number of food crops,
rotation complexity, harvest timing spread), and revenue diversity (income stream
variety, seasonal distribution, market channels, value-added products). Measured
through annual diversity index calculation compared to the farm's own baseline.

Reporting Approach

We are in our lift-oG phase, measuring extensively but reporting what makes sense and

research. As we scale, we expand our reporting capacity. We commit to third-party
veriﬁcation of key claims, adaptive learning loops that translate data into improved
practices, and transparent communication with stakeholders.
