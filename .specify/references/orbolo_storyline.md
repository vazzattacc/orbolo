Orbolo — the storyline on one page
What the website says, in the order it says it. Every page is one beat; every beat resolves a doubt before it
makes an offer.

1. There is a place where beef is still raised on grass, at scale. Paraguay exports around 490,000 tonnes
of beef a year to Chile, Taiwan, the US, Israel and beyond. This is an established industry with working
logistics and sanitary status, not a frontier experiment.

2. The market is rewarding it. Export volumes are falling and prices are rising: fewer animals, more value per
animal. Grass doesn't buy feed, so when grain, energy and freight spike, pasture economics hold. Healthy soil
is the insurance on the asset; it keeps animals gaining through a dry year.

3. Most capital can't get in. Land and cattle still cost a fraction of Uruguay or Argentina, but there has been
no structure for an outside investor to own animals safely and have a guaranteed buyer at the end.

4. We built the structure: the loop. Investors fund a herd → our Paraguayan ranches raise it under the
Orbolo Code → Orbolo's trading desk sells the beef → returns are paid and margin funds the next herd. The
trading desk gives every herd a buyer before it is bought; the herds give the trading desk supply it controls.
Orbolo facilitates and trades; the Paraguayan operating companies raise the cattle and are the contracting
party.

5. We sell at conventional prices. We don't need a premium to make this work. The better product is how we
intend to shift what conventional beef looks like: resilience and scale, not a niche.

6. We're honest about where we are. Today's beef is grass-fed under the MPCS standard, sourced from the
market, without full animal history yet, because traceability is being built first. A 300,000-head producer
alliance is being aligned. No investor cycle has closed yet. Every rule in the Code has a name next to it, and
everything we don't do yet is written on the page.

7. Here is the next ranch. Ranch 04, Chaco Central, 2,400 ha, 400 head needed, 260 under signed letter of
intent. A ranch is released for deployment at 60% committed and fills gradually to 100%. No capital is called
before release. You never fund alone.

8. Here is how you get in. Investors: get the pack, sign a non-binding LOI, receive the contract from the
Paraguayan entity when the ranch is released, get your tag numbers, visit your cattle, get paid at cycle end
and first pick on the next ranch. Buyers: trade with us now at market prices and stand first in line for the
traceable, aligned supply when it comes online. Landowners: bring a ranch into the system. Everyone: come
and see El Tapir.
