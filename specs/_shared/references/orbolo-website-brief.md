Part A — What we are building
1. The company and how it is structured
Orbolo is the facilitator, marketing and communication company for a group that raises and trades beef in
Paraguay. The structure matters for every word on the site:
• Orbolo (Austria) facilitates, markets and communicates. Orbolo does not collect investments and does
not hold investor capital.
• Orbolo's Paraguayan subsidiaries implement the cattle raising, hold the ranch and herd operations,
and are the contracting parties for herd ownership. Investors contract with, and pay, the Paraguayan
entity — never Orbolo.
• Orbolo trades beef. The beef trading desk is Orbolo's own commercial activity and can be described
as such.
The site therefore has two engines under one brand:
1. Beef trading (Orbolo) — grass-fed Paraguayan beef, sourced from the market today, sold to export
buyers at conventional market prices; a traceable, aligned supply chain is being built.
2. Remote Rancher (operated by the Paraguayan subsidiaries, facilitated by Orbolo) — investors
own cattle raised on partner ranches under the Orbolo Code; the subsidiaries manage everything and
sell the animals into Orbolo's trading desk.
Language rules that follow from the structure: - "Orbolo facilitates", "Orbolo connects", "Orbolo markets",
"Orbolo trades beef" — yes. - "Orbolo manages your investment", "invest with Orbolo", "Orbolo raises your
cattle" — no. Use "the Orbolo group", "our Paraguayan operating company [name]", or "Remote Rancher".
- Every investor-facing page carries a footer line naming the contracting entity, to be supplied by counsel. -
Product name: Remote Rancher. Verdant is retired.
2. Goals
• Reposition from "nature-based projects NGO" to a commercial group with a strong ethical code.
• Make the opportunity explicit and the trust case verifiable, being honest about what operates today
versus what is being built.
• Serve investors, beef buyers and landowners with separate paths.
• A coded static site, multilingual, EU-hosted, with a provider-run data room under the Orbolo domain.

5. Audiences and funnels
Investor funnel
1. Awareness — conferences, LinkedIn, DACH networks, referrals, blog.
2. Interest — Invest page → "Get the investor pack" → light data room (email verification, instant, works
on a phone). Follow-up emails, 3–5 over two weeks.
3. Evaluation — call (Google Calendar appointment) → NDA accepted in the data room → full data room
with ranch identities and documents.
4. Letter of intent — non-binding LOI for a specific ranch (head count, ticket, expiry). Signed from a link
inside the data room. This is the only thing that moves the pipeline bar.
5. Commitment — when a ranch reaches 60% of its threshold under signed LOI, the ranch is released for
deployment: LOI signers receive the definitive contract from the Paraguayan entity, capital is called by

9. The opportunity narrative
Seven arguments, each with the proof beside it. Sources live in the data room and are cited on the page.
1. Paraguay is an established exporter. Around 490,000 t cwe projected exports in 2026 to Chile,
Taiwan, the US, Israel and others. The group adds a controlled, verifiable supply layer to a working
industry. Proof: SENACSA/USDA reports; named partners.
2. The market favours asset owners. Q1 2026 export volume fell ~31% while revenue fell ~12%; prices
are up. Fewer animals, more value per animal. Proof: 5-year volume vs value chart, updated quarterly.
3. Grass is the hedge. No feed purchases; the cost base is sun, rain and management. Proof: cost per
kg liveweight vs feedlot benchmark.
4. Ecosystem health is the insurance on the asset, not an income line. Soil, ground cover and water
retention keep animals gaining through a dry year. Carbon or biodiversity credits, where they exist, stay
with the land and the group; the investor's return is beef. Proof: dated baseline measurements per
ranch; claim only what is measured.
5. Built for climate variability. Ground cover, mixed native pasture, and two ecologies (humid east, dry
Chaco). Proof: El Tapir data across at least one bad season.
6. Early entry, real assets, never alone. Land and cattle priced below Uruguay, Argentina, Brazil; stable
tax regime; little international capital yet. A tagged animal you can visit; release only at 60%
committed. Proof: tag number, ranch, visit, the LOI rule.
7. Competitive at conventional prices; the Code is the moat. We do not need a premium to work. The
Code is what lets the group scale a better product inside the conventional market.

Part B — Design, voice and copy
B1. Design direction
The feeling: a serious trading house that happens to be outdoors. Not green startup, not impact fund, not
ranch tourism.
Layout: sparse, generous white space, one idea per screen on mobile. Alternate full-bleed photo →
dense fact block → photo → statement. Numbers as a visual element: large tabular figures with date and
source beneath. Editorial hierarchy; no icon grids.
Typography: headlines in an authoritative serif (paid: Tiempos, GT Sectra, Freight Display; free:
Fraunces, Source Serif 4). Body and numbers in a clean grotesk with tabular figures (paid: Söhne; free:
Inter, IBM Plex Sans; font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums). Two weights per family. Sentence case.
Colour: warm off-white paper (#F6F3EC) and near-black ink (#1C1B18). One earth accent (dry-grass ochre
or Chaco clay) for the single primary button per screen and the loop. Deep muted green (#2E4A34 range)
for Code marks and "verified by". No gradients, pastels or shadows; hairline rules, flat surfaces.
Photography: real animals on real pasture, wide horizons, low sun; ear tags in close-up; people doing the
work; the trading side (cold store, boxed beef, container loading); consistent warm, slightly desaturated
grade; no phone snaps; anonymised ranches unidentifiable; every photo captioned. Plan one half-day
shoot at El Tapir and one at the processor.
Signature components: loop diagram (single continuous line, accent colour, animates once); the Code
as a numbered certificate with "verified by" lines; ledger-style ranch cards; the offer box; dotted-underline
glossary tooltips; roadmap timeline with dated done items; number-unit-date-source as one block.
Mobile: sticky bottom bar with Invest / Beef; ranch cards as horizontal swipe; key-number tables collapse
to stacked cards; test on a mid-range Android and an iPhone.
B2. Voice and copy rules
1. Second person, present tense. "You own", "our ranches raise", "we sell."
2. Verbs over nouns. Short sentences. One claim per sentence. Number → source → date.
3. Concrete over abstract: head counts, hectares, ranch codes, dates, names.
4. Resolve the fear before the upside: Is this real? Who buys the animal? What if not enough people join?
What if it goes wrong? Then returns.
5. Say what we don't do yet, prominently.
6. Banned outside the Glossary: sustainable, holistic, impact, journey, solution, ecosystem, unlock,
seamless, leverage, empower, passionate, premium (as a price claim).
7. Never "regenerative" for today's beef. Never "guaranteed" for returns. Never "invest with Orbolo".
8. Target ranges only until a cycle has closed. Where something can't be proven, say what is being done
about it instead.
9. Long copy is fine on Invest and Beef; sub-heads must tell the story alone.
